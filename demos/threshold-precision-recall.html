The decision threshold: precision, recall and imbalance

Moving the decision threshold trades precision against recall, and the second slider shows how a rare positive class can leave accuracy looking excellent while precision is very poor.

Things worth trying

  1. Set the positive class to 2 percent and leave the threshold at 0.50. Accuracy stays around 80 percent while precision falls below 10 percent, which means that most of the positive predictions are wrong. On imbalanced data, accuracy on its own is misleading.
  2. Keeping the positive class at 2 percent, lower the threshold until recall passes 0.9. Precision falls further, and the F1 score now describes the situation far better than accuracy does.
  3. Raise the threshold to 0.8. Recall falls sharply while specificity climbs toward 1. This is the trade between precision and recall, and no threshold escapes it.
  4. Set the positive class to 50 percent and compare a threshold of 0.50 with one of 0.35. When the classes are balanced, accuracy is a reasonable summary. When they are not, it is not.
  5. Watch precision at a very low threshold, around 0.05. Almost everything is predicted positive, so precision falls to roughly the share of positive examples in the data.
