Week 8 of 10

Optimization and Neural Networks

Gradient descent, learning rates, minibatches, small multilayer networks, and keeping their overfitting under control.

Practical session 8 Homework 6 Final examination, at the end of the course

What this module covers

  • Gradient descent: the direction it moves in, the size of its steps, and what convergence means
  • The learning rate seen across its whole range: too small, well chosen, too large, and divergent
  • Minibatches, and the stochastic gradients they produce
  • The multilayer perceptron: its architecture, the rectified linear activation, and the logarithmic loss
  • The training loop, and the loss functions it minimizes
  • Overfitting in a neural network, and the three remedies for it: narrower layers, weight decay, and stopping early

What you will be able to do afterwards

By the end of this module you should be able to:

  • explain how the learning rate decides whether gradient descent converges at all
  • train a small network on an ordinary processor with a training loop you wrote yourself
  • recognize overfitting in a neural network and apply a remedy that matches it
  • explain what minibatches change, compared with computing the gradient over the whole dataset
