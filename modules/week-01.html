Week 1 of 10

What Does It Mean to Learn?

What it means to learn from examples: baselines, the discipline of the training and test split, and the first signs of data leakage.

Practical session 1 No homework this week Midterm examination, after Module 5

What this module covers

  • Learning as the discovery of a pattern in examples, which is what supervised learning does
  • Features, targets, and the function that connects them; how to state a learning problem precisely
  • Baselines, and why no model can be judged without one to compare it against
  • Splitting the data into a training part and a test part, and why the test part is used exactly once
  • The difference between generalizing and memorizing
  • The first signs of data leakage, and why it is so dangerous

What you will be able to do afterwards

By the end of this module you should be able to:

  • explain what learning means in this context, and how it differs from memorizing
  • state a supervised learning problem, naming the features and the target
  • design a simple experiment: a baseline, a correct split, and an honest evaluation
  • recognize the simplest forms of data leakage in somebody else's code
