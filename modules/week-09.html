Week 9 of 10

Diagnosing Machine-Learning Systems

Learning curves, the analysis of errors, permutation importance, and what happens when the data shifts.

Practical session 9 No homework this week Final examination, at the end of the course

What this module covers

  • Learning curves, and how they separate bias from variance by varying the amount of data
  • The analysis of errors: which mistakes the model makes, and what those mistakes have in common
  • Permutation importance, and what correlated features do to it
  • Shifts in the distribution: training on data of one kind and testing on data of another
  • A systematic procedure for answering the question of what exactly is wrong with a model

What you will be able to do afterwards

By the end of this module you should be able to:

  • read a learning curve and say whether bias or variance dominates
  • carry out an analysis of errors, identifying which classes and which examples the model confuses
  • use permutation importance, and explain where it stops being trustworthy
  • detect a shift in the distribution of the data and propose a response to it
