Week 3 of 10

Learning a Boundary (Classification)

Learning a boundary between classes: logistic regression, nearest neighbors, confusion matrices, metrics, and thresholds.

Practical session 3 Homework 2 Midterm examination, after Module 5

What this module covers

  • Classification understood as learning a boundary between the classes
  • Logistic regression and nearest neighbors, two quite different ways of drawing that boundary
  • The confusion matrix, and the four counts it contains: true positives, false positives, false negatives and true negatives
  • Accuracy, precision, recall, specificity and the F1 score, and what each of them measures
  • The decision threshold, and choosing the point at which the model will operate
  • Imbalance between the classes, and the two curves that describe it: the receiver operating characteristic and the precision-recall curve

What you will be able to do afterwards

By the end of this module you should be able to:

  • compute a confusion matrix and interpret every metric derived from it
  • explain why accuracy misleads when one class is rare
  • choose a decision threshold for a stated structure of costs
  • compare the two kinds of curve, and say what changes when the positive class becomes rarer
