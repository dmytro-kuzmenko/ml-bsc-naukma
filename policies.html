Course policies

Artificial-intelligence tools · oral checks · collaboration · late submissions · academic integrity

Using artificial-intelligence tools

  • For homework assignments and for the mini-project, generative artificial-intelligence tools, coding assistants, documentation, search engines and other online resources are all permitted unless a particular task says otherwise.
  • The submitted work remains your responsibility. You must understand the code, the methodological decisions, the analysis and the conclusions, and you must be able to explain them out loud.
  • For the individual assessments, that is the midterm and the final examination, these tools and any other outside help are prohibited, unless the instructor explicitly allows something different for a particular examination.
  • You do not have to log every interaction. What you must provide is a substantive disclosure: which tools you used, what kind of help they gave you, a few representative prompts, and which parts of the work you verified or changed yourself. If you made heavy use of a tool, keep the representative prompts or an export of the conversation, because the instructor may ask to see them.
  • This course does not use software that claims to detect artificial-intelligence writing, and no piece of work is ever labeled as machine-generated on the basis of its style. Academic integrity is protected by verifying understanding instead.

Oral checks

  • Any piece of submitted work may be selected for a short oral check of two to five minutes, conducted on the submission itself.
  • The usual procedure is to open the submission, explain one part of the work, justify a methodological choice, and answer a single question about what would change under different circumstances or about what a result means.
  • Students are selected at random, with priority given to those who have not yet been checked. Every student can expect to be called at least once during the semester, and any student may be called more than once. The instructor may also start a check directly, without relying on any detection software, and you are welcome to ask for a check yourself.
  • There are four possible outcomes. If you show that the work is yours, the grade does not change. If a small gap appears, you receive brief feedback and the grade does not change. If you prove substantially unable to explain what you submitted, that component may be reassessed under the course and university regulations. If the evidence indicates that the work is not your own, the matter goes to the academic-integrity procedure.
  • An oral check is never a separate grade.

Working with others

  • Study groups, discussion of concepts and of general strategy, and preparing for the examinations together are all encouraged.
  • Four things are prohibited: copying directly, sharing the final version of your code, submitting an analysis written by somebody else, and, in a pair, leaving almost all of the joint work to one of the two people.

Late submissions

  • Every student has five late days to spread across all six homework assignments as they wish. One late day covers up to 24 hours, and several may be spent on a single assignment without asking permission first.
  • Once the late days are used up, the maximum grade falls by 25 percent for every further 24 hours, unless a different arrangement has been agreed on the basis of documented valid reasons.
  • The mini-project and the examinations may be governed by separate rules.

Academic integrity

All work in this course is governed by the Regulations on Academic Integrity for Students of the National University of Kyiv-Mohyla Academy, issued as Order No. 112 of 7 March 2018.

Questions about how the grade is calculated are answered on the assessment page, and the rules that govern the assignments themselves are on the homework page.
