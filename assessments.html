Assessment

30 points of homework, 15 for the midterm, 25 for the mini-project, 30 for the final examination · concluding in a pass or fail credit

At least 30 points on the university scale are required before you may sit the final assessment.

The midterm examination

Format. An individual test on DistEdu lasting about 30 minutes, taken after Module 5. The questions test conceptual and methodological reasoning. None of them asks you to recite a definition, work through a long derivation, or write substantial code.

What it tests. Reading a plot or a table, recognizing underfitting and overfitting, choosing a metric, choosing a validation strategy, finding data leakage, and predicting what a change to one parameter would do.

The rules. Artificial-intelligence tools and help from other people are prohibited; see the course policies.

Sample midterm questions

The final examination

Format. A cumulative test on DistEdu lasting about 45 minutes and covering all ten modules. Roughly 20 to 25 percent of the marks go to short conceptual reasoning, 50 to 60 percent to interpretation and diagnosis, and 20 to 25 percent to methodological decisions. There is very little arithmetic.

The rules. Artificial-intelligence tools and help from other people are prohibited; see the course policies.

Sample final-examination questions

Oral checks

An oral check carries no points of its own. Any piece of submitted work may be selected for a short conversation of two to five minutes, in which you explain one part of the work, justify a methodological choice, and answer a single question about how a change would affect the result or about what the result means. Selection is random, and priority goes to students who have not yet been called. Every student can expect to be called at least once during the semester, and any student may be called more than once. The instructor may also start a check directly, and you may ask for one yourself. The course policies describe the procedure and its outcomes in full.
